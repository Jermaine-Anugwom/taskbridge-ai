# Field notes

## Discovery questions

Start with the work rather than a preferred tool.

1. What event starts the task?
2. Who performs each step, including unofficial workarounds?
3. Which systems and documents are used?
4. Which decisions are rules and which require judgment?
5. What information is commonly missing or contradictory?
6. What happens when the task goes wrong?
7. How often does it occur and how much touch time does it require?
8. Which data is sensitive, regulated, or restricted?
9. Can a wrong action be reversed?
10. Who has authority over the final outcome?

## Intervention test

- Prefer deterministic automation when inputs are structured, rules are stable, and repeatability matters.
- Consider AI assistance when language is central, results are reversible, and people can inspect the suggestion.
- Use a hybrid when a predictable common path coexists with consequential exceptions.
- Leave the workflow alone when volume is low, the process is unstable, or the improvement cannot repay its operating cost.

## Pilot constraints

- Begin read-only.
- Preserve source evidence with every recommendation.
- Name each human checkpoint and owner.
- Measure the baseline before claiming improvement.
- Keep a documented manual path and rollback trigger.
- Ask employees what became harder, not only what became faster.

## Handoff checklist

- Process owner confirmed
- Inputs, outputs, and source systems documented
- Exception examples reviewed
- Data boundary approved
- Decision authority explicit
- Measures and stop conditions agreed
- Rollback tested
- Training language reviewed by the people doing the work
